About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Chemistry at KAIST, working on surface-engineered nanomaterials, metal–semiconductor interfaces, and in situ/operando characterization for photoelectrochemical energy conversion. My research focuses on understanding how surface composition, interface structure, and charge transfer affect catalytic and photoelectrochemical reactivity.

Research

Plasmonic Au@Pd Antenna–Reactor Nanoparticles

Investigated hot carrier dynamics and photoelectrochemical oxygen evolution on Au@Pd/TiO2 nanostructures. Combined nanoparticle synthesis, TiO2 nanotube electrode fabrication, PEC measurements, FDTD simulation, and transient absorption spectroscopy.

Metal–Semiconductor Schottky Interfaces

Fabricated Schottky nanodiodes using e-beam evaporation and analyzed how metal type, thickness, and thermal treatment affect I–V characteristics, Schottky barrier behavior, and interfacial charge transfer.

In Situ XAFS Analysis of Photoelectrochemical Catalysts

Designed and built an in situ PEC-XAFS experimental setup at the PAL 10C Wide XAFS beamline to monitor changes in oxidation state and coordination environment under reaction conditions.

Facet-Controlled Oxide Nanomaterials

Studied how exposed crystal facets of Cu2O and TiO2 influence interfacial charge transfer, electrochemical reactivity, and charge trapping behavior.
